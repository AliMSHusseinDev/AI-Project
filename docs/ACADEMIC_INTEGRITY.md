# AI assistance and academic integrity

This project package was drafted and implemented with substantial help from an OpenAI coding assistant, at the user's request. This includes the code, tests, synthetic terrain files, experiment scripts, documentation, and proposed role plan. The experiment numbers came from running the included code. The work was not copied from a public sensor-placement repository.

The six students' actual contributions are not known. Names, IDs, contribution evidence, and TA approval are therefore left blank. The role table is a plan for study and review, not a record of work already completed by students.

The course says students may only submit their own work. It does not say whether this level of AI assistance is allowed. Before submission, ask the TA what use is permitted. Do not submit this package as proof that six students independently wrote it.

Each team member should understand the model, run the program, check tests and results, read a paper, and explain a change. Record actual work in `TEAM.md`. Do not invent a development history, fake commits under classmates' names, or claim that the instructor has approved the project.

The literature review uses six cited academic references. Reference details were checked against Crossref. Bibliographic checks do not mean all full papers were accessed. Source access limits are recorded in `sources/README.md`. Summaries are written in simple words and do not claim to reproduce published experiments.

Suggested disclosure to adapt after checking the course rules:

> We used an AI coding assistant to help draft the initial implementation, tests, experiment tools, and documentation. Our actual review, changes, experiments, and individual contributions are listed in the contribution record. We have followed the TA's instructions on permitted AI use.
